# Stock Trading Reference Guide

Last updated: 2026-06-06

---

## 1. Before You Trade

### Mindset
- Only trade money you will not need for at least 3 years
- Profitable trading is boring — if you feel excited, that is a warning sign, not a green light
- Keep a trading journal: record your entry reason, exit reason, and how you felt. Review weekly.
- Never trade on tips, social media hype, or FOMO. If you are chasing a spike, you are already late.

### Have a plan before every trade
Every trade needs these defined **before** you enter:
- Entry price and reason
- Stop-loss price (your maximum acceptable loss)
- Take-profit target
- Position size (how many shares)

If you cannot answer all four, do not enter the trade.

## 2. Reading a Stock — What to Look At

### Fundamental Analysis (the "why" behind the price)
Fundamentals tell you whether the underlying business is healthy and worth owning.

| What to check | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Revenue growth YoY | Is the business actually growing? |
| Gross / operating margin | Is it profitable and efficient? |
| EPS and earnings beat/miss | Did it meet, beat, or disappoint expectations? |
| Forward P/E | How expensive is it relative to expected earnings? |
| Debt-to-Equity ratio | Is it over-leveraged? |
| Free cash flow | Is it generating real cash, not just accounting profit? |
| Analyst consensus + price targets | What do professionals expect? |

**Red flags:**
- Declining margins over multiple quarters
- Revenue growing but cash flow shrinking (poor cash conversion)
- Rising debt with no revenue growth to justify it
- Frequent "adjusted" or non-GAAP earnings adjustments
- Single customer accounting for >10% of revenue

**A cheap price does not mean undervalued.** Low price can reflect outdated products, bad management, litigation risk, or expiring patents.

### Technical Analysis (the "when" to enter/exit)
Technicals tell you what the market is doing right now, not what the company is worth.

| Indicator | What to look for |
|---|---|
| 50-day MA | Short-term trend. Price above = bullish momentum. |
| 200-day MA | Long-term trend. Price above = overall uptrend. |
| RSI (14-day) | >70 = overbought (consider trimming). <30 = oversold (potential entry). 30–70 = healthy range. |
| MACD | Bullish crossover (MACD crosses above signal) = momentum building. Bearish crossover = weakening. |
| Volume | Rising volume on up-days confirms buyers. Rising volume on down-days confirms sellers. Low volume moves are less reliable. |

**Combined approach:** Use fundamentals to pick the company, use technicals to pick the entry/exit timing.

## 3. Risk Management

### The 1–2% Rule
Never risk more than 1–2% of your total portfolio on a single trade.

Example: $10,000 portfolio → maximum risk per trade = $100–$200.

### Position Sizing Formula
```
Shares to buy = Risk per trade ($) / Risk per share ($)

Risk per share = Entry price − Stop-loss price
```

Example:
- Portfolio: $10,000 → risk 1% = $100
- Entry price: $50 | Stop-loss: $47 → risk per share = $3
- Shares to buy: $100 / $3 = 33 shares

This keeps any single loss capped at $100 regardless of how wrong you are.

### Stop-Loss Placement
- Set it at the **moment you enter** — never rely on mental stops
- Place below a key support level or moving average, not at an arbitrary round number
- A wider stop does not mean more risk if you size down proportionally
- For volatile stocks, use ATR (Average True Range) to set a stop that gives the stock room to breathe

### Risk-to-Reward Ratio
Aim for at least **2:1** — for every $1 you risk, target $2 in profit.

At 2:1, you only need to be right 40% of the time to be profitable.

### Portfolio-level rules
- Cap individual position at 20–25% of portfolio (concentration risk)
- If two or more stocks are in the same sector, count them together toward that cap
- Hold some cash — it lets you act when opportunities appear without forcing you to sell something else

## 4. Reading the Market Context

Before placing any trade, understand what the broader market is doing.

### Macro factors to monitor
- **Fed interest rate decisions** — rising rates hurt growth stocks (high P/E); falling rates help them
- **CPI / PCE (inflation)** — high inflation → Fed stays hawkish → market headwind
- **NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls)** — strong jobs data = healthy economy but also potential Fed tightening
- **Earnings season** — even good companies can drop if the sector is getting re-rated

### Risk-on vs Risk-off
- **Risk-on**: investors favour growth stocks, tech, small caps. VIX is low.
- **Risk-off**: investors flee to bonds, gold, defensive stocks (utilities, consumer staples). VIX is high.

Knowing the regime helps you set expectations — a stock that would normally hold after bad news may sell off hard in a risk-off environment.

## 5. News and Catalysts

### High-impact events (can move a stock 5–20% in a day)
- Earnings report: beat/miss vs expectations, and especially the **guidance** (forward outlook matters more than the past quarter)
- Analyst upgrade or downgrade with a new price target
- CEO/CFO change or large insider buying/selling (>$1M)
- Regulatory approval or rejection (especially for biotech/pharma)
- Product launch that opens a new revenue stream
- Macro policy that directly affects the sector (tariffs, rate decisions)

### Lower-impact (watch but don't panic)
- Minor analyst note with no price target change
- Sector-wide selloff from a peer company's earnings (contagion)
- General market volatility with no company-specific news

**Rule:** Before reacting to news, ask — does this change the investment thesis, or is it noise?

## 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No stop-loss | One bad trade wipes out weeks of gains | Set stop before entering |
| Holding a loser "waiting to break even" | The breakeven price may never come | Respect your stop, cut losses early |
| Chasing a stock after a spike | You are buying someone else's gains | Wait for a pullback or skip it |
| Overtrading | Commissions and bad entries accumulate | Only trade setups that meet all your criteria |
| Averaging down blindly | Throwing good money after bad | Only add to a position if the thesis is intact, not just because the price fell |
| Over-diversifying | You cannot monitor 30 positions effectively | Aim for 5–15 well-understood positions |
| Ignoring macro context | A good stock in a bad market still falls | Check regime before entry |
| Trading on emotion | Fear and greed destroy discipline | Follow the plan you wrote before entering |

## 7. Hold vs Sell Decision Framework

### Sell signals
- RSI > 75 and price near 52-week high (overbought)
- Negative earnings surprise + price dropped >5% on the day
- Analyst consensus turns to Sell/Strong Sell
- High-impact negative news that changes the investment thesis
- Unrealised gain >40% and MACD shows bearish crossover
- Price fell >8% in 5 days with no sector-wide explanation

**2+ signals → seriously consider selling. 3+ signals → sell.**

### Hold signals
- Price above 50-day MA and 200-day MA
- RSI in the 30–70 healthy range
- MACD neutral or bullish
- Analyst consensus is Buy or Hold with >10% upside to target
- No high-impact negative news
- Business moat and fundamentals still intact

**5+ signals → hold with confidence.**

## 8. Quick Reference Checklist (per trade)

Before entering:
- [ ] Why am I buying this? (1 sentence thesis)
- [ ] What are the fundamentals saying?
- [ ] What are the technicals saying?
- [ ] What is my stop-loss price?
- [ ] What is my take-profit target?
- [ ] How many shares does the 1–2% rule allow?
- [ ] Is this position within my sector concentration limit?
- [ ] What macro events are coming in the next 2 weeks that could affect this?

After entering:
- [ ] Log the trade in journal (entry reason, entry price, stop, target)
- [ ] Set price alerts at stop-loss and take-profit levels
- [ ] Review fundamentals after each earnings report
- [ ] Re-assess if high-impact news changes the thesis

## 10. Algorithmic and Automated Trading Tools

These let you backtest strategies on historical data before risking real money — the most important step before automating anything.

### Backtesting and Strategy Building

| Tool | What it does | Skill level |
|---|---|---|
| [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com) | Open-source, multi-asset algo trading. Write strategies in Python/C#, backtest on decades of data, connect directly to brokers. Industry standard. | Intermediate–Advanced |
| [TradingView Pine Script](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/) | Write custom indicators and strategy backtests directly in TradingView. Easiest starting point for coding strategies. | Beginner–Intermediate |
| [NautilusTrader](https://nautilustrader.io) | High-performance Python/Rust trading engine. Best for serious backtesting speed and live deployment. | Advanced |
| [PyAlgoTrade](https://github.com/gbeced/pyalgotrade) | Python library for backtesting stock strategies. Good for learning algo concepts without complexity. | Intermediate |
| [Arrow Algo](https://arrowalgo.com) | Write strategies in plain English — no coding required. Converts to backtestable logic automatically. | Beginner |

### How to use these without coding (TradingView approach)
1. Open a chart on TradingView
2. Click "Indicators" → search community scripts (thousands of free strategies)
3. Apply a strategy script → it shows historical buy/sell signals on the chart
4. Open "Strategy Tester" tab → see win rate, profit factor, drawdown on historical data
5. If backtested well, watch for those signals in real time before manually placing trades

**Golden rule of backtesting:** A strategy that looks great in backtests often fails live because of overfitting (it was tuned to past data). Always forward-test in paper trading for at least 1–3 months before trading real money.

## 11. Skills to Build (in order of priority)

### Level 1 — Foundation (do these first)
- **Read financial statements** — learn to read an income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. Free course: [Khan Academy Finance](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/core-finance)
- **Understand the indicators** — know what RSI, MACD, and moving averages actually calculate, not just what colours mean. [Investopedia indicator guides](https://www.investopedia.com/technical-analysis-4689657) cover each one clearly.
- **Keep a trading journal** — track every trade: date, ticker, entry, exit, reason, outcome. Review weekly for patterns in your own behaviour.

### Level 2 — Intermediate
- **Earnings analysis** — learn to read an earnings call transcript, not just the headline EPS number. Guidance and tone matter more than the past quarter.
- **Sector rotation** — understand which sectors lead in different macro phases (e.g. tech leads in rate-cut cycles, energy leads in inflation). [Fidelity sector tracker](https://eresearch.fidelity.com/eresearch/markets_sectors/sectors/sectors_in_market.jhtml) is free.
- **Options basics** — not to trade options, but to understand implied volatility (IV). High IV = market expects a big move; useful context before earnings.

### Level 3 — Advanced
- **Pine Script on TradingView** — code and backtest your own entry/exit rules. No prior coding experience needed.
- **Python for finance** — use pandas and yfinance to pull data, calculate indicators, and test ideas programmatically. More flexible than TradingView.
- **QuantConnect** — build a fully automated strategy, backtest on real historical data, and eventually deploy to a live broker.
